# Updates: November 4, 2026

This update makes the site easier to use on phones and tablets. It also lets visitors search for churches by address on the Support a parish page, with suggestions as they type.

## Search for churches by address

### What was added

The Support a parish page now has a search box under the map. Visitors can type an address, a city, or a ZIP code. When they tap Search or press Enter, the map shows the Catholic churches near that place. This uses the same church search as the Find churches near me button.

### Why

Find churches near me only works when a visitor shares their location. Some people prefer not to share it. On some devices the button doesn't work at all. Before this change, those visitors could only leave the site and use Google Maps. Now they can search right on the page.

### How it works

The address is looked up with Nominatim. Nominatim is the free address search run by OpenStreetMap. The site already uses OpenStreetMap for its maps and its church search, so this keeps everything in one place. It needs no account, no key, and no payment.

The address goes from the visitor's browser straight to OpenStreetMap. It is never sent to this site's server, and it is never saved.

### What visitors see

The search box has a clear label, so it works well with screen readers.

The results list shows the place the search used. For example, it might say "Near: Chicago, Illinois, United States." This lets visitors check that the right place was found. On the map, that point is labeled "The address you entered."

Sometimes an address can't be found. When that happens, the page suggests adding the city and state, or trying a ZIP code.

If a visitor chooses not to share their location, the page now points them to the address box. Before, it only pointed them to Google Maps.

The privacy note under the map now explains how a typed address is used.

### Following OpenStreetMap's rules

Nominatim has a usage policy, and the site follows it.

The policy allows at most one search per second. The site waits a little over one second between searches.

The policy does not allow searching while someone is still typing. The site only searches when the visitor taps Search or presses Enter.

The policy asks sites to credit OpenStreetMap. The Sources page now lists Nominatim under the OpenStreetMap entry.

### Good to know

A full street address gives the most accurate results. A ZIP code alone is less exact. In one test, the ZIP code 60601 in downtown Chicago was placed about 10 miles west of downtown. The church search covers about 9 miles around the point it finds. So with only a ZIP code, the results may lean toward one side of the area.

## Address suggestions while you type

### What was added

The address box now suggests places as you type. After three letters or numbers, a list of up to five matches appears under the box. Picking one starts the church search right away. Visitors can still type a full address and tap Search without picking anything.

The list works with a keyboard. The up and down arrows move through the suggestions. Enter picks one, and Escape closes the list. Screen readers announce how many suggestions are showing.

### Why

Typing a full address is slow, especially on a phone. Suggestions save time. They also help visitors catch a typo before they search.

### How it works

Nominatim does not allow suggestions while someone types. So the suggestions come from Photon instead. Photon is a free address search built by Komoot. It uses the same OpenStreetMap data as the rest of the map.

The site waits for a short pause in typing before it asks Photon for suggestions. It also skips requests it no longer needs and reuses answers it already has. This keeps the site within Photon's fair use.

Suggestions leave out shops, restaurants, and other businesses. They show addresses, streets, towns, and ZIP codes. Places in the visitor's own country show first. The site works out the country from the browser's language setting and uses the United States if it can't tell.

What a visitor types goes only to Photon. It is never sent to this site's server.

### Good to know

Photon is free, but it comes with no promise that it will always be available. If it stops answering, the suggestions simply won't appear. The Search button still works as before.

## Clearer loading and more complete church results

### What changed

The church search now shows that it's working. Gray placeholder cards appear where the results will go, with a line saying what the site is doing. If the search takes more than a few seconds, a note explains that it can take up to a minute. The address box also shows "Looking for matches…" while it fetches suggestions.

The church list now finds more churches. It shows the 3 closest, and visitors can scroll the list to see the rest. A sliver of the next church peeks out at the bottom, so it's clear there are more.

Under the results, a note invites visitors to add a missing church to OpenStreetMap or to check their diocese's parish directory.

### Why

The search often takes 30 seconds or more. OpenStreetMap's church search is a free, shared service, and it slows down when many sites use it at once. Before this change the page just said "Searching…" on the button, so it was easy to think it had stopped working.

Some churches were missing because of a mistake in how the site asked for results. It asked for only 80 churches, and the search service picks those 80 in its own order, not by distance. In a busy city like Chicago there are more than 170 Catholic churches in range, so some of the closest ones were left out. The site now gets all of them and keeps the nearest 40.

The search now also includes the Eastern Catholic Churches, such as Ukrainian Greek Catholic, Maronite, and Melkite parishes. These churches are in full communion with Rome. Old Catholic and Anglo-Catholic churches are not, so they are still left out.

### Good to know

OpenStreetMap is built by volunteers. A church only shows up if someone has added it to the map and marked it as Catholic. Some churches are on the map without that label, so they still won't appear. The site doesn't guess from names, because many churches of other traditions are also named after saints.

## A new layout for Support a parish

### What changed

On wide screens, the church search now sits beside the map, where the Catholic charities used to be. The buttons, the address box, and the results are all on the right. The map is on the left. It grows to match the height of the search side, so there's no empty space under it. The results sit inside a box with a blue border, so they're easy to spot on the page.

The note about how your location and address are used stays under the search until you start your first search. Then it moves to the bottom of the page. Visitors still see it before they share anything, and the results get more room afterward.

The Catholic charities now appear at the bottom of the page. Catholic Charities of New York has been added to the list, next to Catholic Relief Services and Catholic Charities USA.

On smaller screens, the map comes first and the search sits below it.

### Why

Finding a church is the main reason people visit this page. Putting the search next to the map lets visitors see the results and the map at the same time. Before, the results appeared under the map, so visitors had to scroll back and forth.

## About page photo on phones and tablets

### What changed

On wide screens, the photo on the About page stays in place beside the story as you scroll. On phones and tablets, the photo now sits at the top of the page and scrolls away like the rest of the page.

### Why

On narrow screens, the photo sits above the story instead of beside it. Because it stayed in place, it covered the text as visitors scrolled. The story was hard to read on phones and tablets. Now the full story can be read without anything in the way.

This applies to screens 1024 pixels wide or smaller. That covers phones, and tablets held either way.

## A simpler header on phones and tablets

### What changed

The "Free, work in progress" label no longer shows in the header on phones and tablets. The header now shows only the site name and the menu button. Wide screens still show the label.

### Why

On small screens the header felt crowded. Removing the label gives the site name and the menu more room.

Visitors can still see that the site is a work in progress. The footer on every page still says the site is free, ad free, and a work in progress.

## Still to check

These changes passed the site's automatic code checks. The address search and the suggestions were tested on their own and found the right places. The new search box and its suggestion list have not yet been tried in a browser. The phone and tablet layouts still need a check on real devices.
